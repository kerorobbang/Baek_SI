Turning Spectra into Environmental Insight

My research sits at the intersection of optical remote sensing and quantitative environmental science. Across drones, aircraft and satellites, I build workflows that convert multispectral and hyperspectral measurements into reliable, physically-grounded estimates — for coastal waters, tidal ecosystems, forests, and engineered structures.

water

Coastal & Ocean Remote Sensing

Coastal and shelf waters are optically complex and change quickly. I use ocean-color and hyperspectral observations — from GOCI-II to UAV and airborne sensors — to quantify harmful algal blooms (red tides), map tidal-flat vegetation and benthic habitats, and track water-quality constituents such as coloured dissolved organic matter. A recurring goal is customized bio-optical modelling that links measured radiance to biologically meaningful quantities.

UAV & Airborne Imaging

Reliable measurements from low-altitude platforms demand careful calibration. I work on radiometric correction of drone-mounted multispectral cameras (e.g. RedEdge-MX) by intercalibration with hyperspectral sensors, improving geometric precision by fusing high-resolution imagery, and correcting bidirectional reflectance (BRDF) effects that vary with forest type and season. The result is airborne data you can trust for quantitative analysis.

Machine Learning for Monitoring

Spectral imagery pairs naturally with data-driven methods. I apply machine learning and deep learning to estimate material properties from hyperspectral data (e.g. TiO₂ content and coarse-aggregate properties in concrete), to segment structures with foundation models such as the Segment Anything Model (SAM), to classify forest-fire damage for the CAS500-4 mission, and to detect change in coastal structures from UAV imagery.
